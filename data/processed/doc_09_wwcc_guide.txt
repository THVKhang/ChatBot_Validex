Working With Children Check (WWCC) is a mandatory screening requirement across all Australian states and territories for individuals who work or volunteer with children. Each jurisdiction administers its own WWCC scheme, though the purpose is consistent: to assess whether a person poses a risk to the safety of children based on their criminal history and other relevant records.

In New South Wales, the WWCC is managed by the Office of the Children's Guardian. Applicants apply online, provide identity verification, and receive either a clearance or a bar. A WWCC clearance in NSW is valid for five years and must be verified by the employer through the online system. Paid workers pay a fee, while volunteers can apply at no cost.

In Victoria, the Working with Children Check is administered by the Department of Justice and Community Safety. A valid Victorian WWCC card is required before commencing child-related work. The card is valid for five years and is linked to the cardholder's criminal record, meaning it can be revoked at any time if new relevant information is recorded.

In Queensland, the Blue Card system serves as the equivalent of the WWCC. Managed by Blue Card Services, it screens individuals working in regulated child-related activities. Processing times vary, but most applications are assessed within four to six weeks. A Blue Card must be renewed before it expires.

Key differences between states include the application fee structure, whether volunteers are exempt, renewal periods, and which offences are classified as disqualifying. Employers must verify that a worker holds a valid WWCC before they begin child-related duties, and must notify the relevant authority if the worker's role changes or if they become aware of relevant criminal charges.

Failure to hold a valid WWCC when required is a criminal offence in most jurisdictions. Employers who knowingly engage an unscreened worker may also face penalties. For organisations operating across multiple states, managing WWCC compliance requires awareness of each jurisdiction's rules and renewal timelines.
