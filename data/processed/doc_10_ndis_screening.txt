The NDIS Worker Screening Check is a nationally consistent assessment required for workers in certain risk-assessed roles within the National Disability Insurance Scheme. The check is designed to determine whether a person poses an unacceptable risk of harm to people with disability.

NDIS worker screening is administered by each state and territory's worker screening unit. The check assesses a person's national criminal history, relevant disciplinary or misconduct findings, and any other relevant information. The outcome is either a clearance or an exclusion.

Workers in risk-assessed roles — those involving more than incidental contact with NDIS participants — must hold a valid NDIS Worker Screening Check clearance before commencing work. This applies to employees, contractors, and volunteers. Registered NDIS providers are required to ensure all workers in these roles are screened.

The NDIS Worker Screening Check is different from a standard national police check. While a police check reveals disclosable criminal history, the NDIS screening involves a risk assessment that considers how the person's history may affect the safety of people with disability. A person with a criminal history may still receive a clearance if the offences are not relevant to disability support work.

Key aspects of the NDIS Worker Screening Check include continuous monitoring, meaning that once a person is cleared, any subsequent changes to their criminal record are automatically assessed. This is a significant difference from a point-in-time police check.

Processing times vary by jurisdiction but generally take two to four weeks for straightforward applications. Complex cases requiring manual assessment may take longer. Workers can apply through their state or territory's worker screening unit, usually via an online portal.

Employers should maintain a register of all workers who require screening, track clearance expiry dates, and have processes for managing interim arrangements when a worker's check is pending.
