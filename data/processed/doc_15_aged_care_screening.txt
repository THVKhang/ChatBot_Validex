Aged care worker screening in Australia is governed by the Aged Care Act 1997 and its associated regulations. All workers in Commonwealth-funded aged care services must undergo a police check before commencing work and at regular intervals thereafter. This requirement was strengthened following the recommendations of the Royal Commission into Aged Care Quality and Safety.

The Aged Care Quality and Safety Commission (ACQSC) oversees compliance with screening requirements for approved providers. Under current legislation, a person is not eligible to work in aged care if they have been convicted of murder or sexual assault. For other offences, the provider must conduct a risk assessment to determine whether the person is suitable for the role.

Police checks for aged care workers are typically required every three years, although some providers implement annual screening as a matter of best practice. The check must be obtained through an ACIC-accredited body and must be a nationally coordinated criminal history check.

In addition to police checks, aged care workers in certain states may also require an NDIS Worker Screening Check if the service provides supports to NDIS participants. This dual requirement means that aged care providers must track both police check validity and NDIS screening status for relevant workers.

Key responsibilities for aged care providers include maintaining a register of all workers and their screening status, ensuring new workers are screened before their first shift with residents, conducting risk assessments for workers with disclosable criminal history, implementing policies for managing workers whose checks are pending or have returned adverse results, and notifying the ACQSC of any reportable incidents related to worker conduct.

The Serious Incident Response Scheme (SIRS) requires aged care providers to report and manage serious incidents, including allegations of criminal conduct by workers. A robust screening program helps prevent such incidents by identifying potential risks before workers commence in roles with vulnerable people.

For workers who hold a current police check from another industry (such as childcare or healthcare), the aged care provider should verify whether the check meets the specific requirements of the aged care sector. Generally, a police check obtained for another purpose is acceptable if it is a nationally coordinated check and is within the provider's accepted validity period.
